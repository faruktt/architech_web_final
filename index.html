The new Tennessee Performing Arts Center (TPAC) – designed by BIG, William Rawn Associates, and HASTINGS Architecture – extends the vibrancy of downtown Nashville across the Cumberland River, emerging as a cultural anchor for the city's reenergized East Bank. Designed to welcome the community from all sides, with the performance venues clustered at its core, the new TPAC supports a range of artistic programming while building upon Nashville's rich legacy in the arts. Construction is expected to begin in 2027, with a projected opening in 2030.

Situated on a waterfront site neighboring Cumberland Park and the Tennessee Titans Nissan Stadium, the new TPAC includes four performance spaces – the multi-function Grand Broadway theater; a dance and opera hall; a flexible black box theater; and an intimate cabaret space – as well as rehearsal studios and classrooms.

NOT A HOTEL Corporation

Completed 2024

■fin✕
NOT A HOTEL Setouchi is a private island retreat nestled in Japan's Seto Inland Sea. The project transforms the existing landscape of Sagishima into a seamless living environment where architecture and nature are inseparable. Drawing from the island's agricultural heritage, the design references the terraced hillsides and stone walls built by generations of islanders.

The complex includes private villas, communal gathering spaces, and a series of outdoor terraces that cascade toward the sea. Materials are sourced locally and the construction employs traditional Japanese craftsmanship alongside contemporary structural engineering.

The Gastronomy Open Ecosystem in San Sebastian is a new cultural institution dedicated to food as art, science, and heritage. Situated on the coastline of the Basque Country, the project creates a network of indoor and outdoor spaces that celebrate the region's extraordinary culinary culture.

Located on a 42-acre site at the NASA Ames Research Center, Google's Bay View campus consists of three buildings totaling 1.1 million square feet and approximately 18 acres of open space. The all-electric campus has achieved a LEED-NC v4 Platinum certification.

On the exterior, the buildings feature a first-of-its-kind "dragonscale" solar skin roof equipped with 50,000 silver solar panels that generate nearly four megawatts of energy, approximately 40% of the buildings' annual energy use. The campus also includes 17.3 acres of high-value natural habitat areas.
CopenHill
Copenhagen, Denmark
CopenHill
Copenhagen, Denmark

Amager Ressourcecenter

Infrastructure / Culture

41,000

Completed 2019

■fin✕
CopenHill – officially known as the Amager Bakke – is a waste-to-energy plant and urban recreation center. The facility is surmounted by a ski slope, hiking trail, and the world's tallest climbing wall – transforming what is typically an industrial facility into a vibrant public amenity for Copenhagen residents.

The plant processes 400,000 tonnes of waste per year and generates enough electricity for 30,000 homes and district heating for 72,000 households. Every 100 kg of CO₂ emitted, a smoke ring is released from the chimney as a reminder of the environmental impact of consumption.

In Progress

■fin✕
Gelephu Mindfulness City is a new city commissioned by His Majesty the King of Bhutan, guided by the principle of Gross National Happiness. Spanning 1,000 km² in southern Bhutan, the city prioritizes ecological health, cultural preservation, and human wellbeing.

With a six-story building height limit, abundant green corridors, and complete reliance on renewable energy, Gelephu demonstrates that the cities of the future can be more humane, more connected to nature, and more equitable than those of the past.

Superkilen
Copenhagen, Denmark

Landscape / Urban

30,000

Completed 2012

■fin✕
Superkilen is a radical public square – a community park exhibiting objects from the many cultures that call the neighborhood home. Spanning over 750m through one of Copenhagen's most ethnically diverse and socially challenged neighborhoods, Superkilen is divided into three zones: The Red Square, The Black Market, and The Green Park.

Each zone includes urban furniture and fixtures gathered from the countries of origin of the neighborhood's residents – from a Yemeni shade pavilion to a Moroccan fountain to Thai boxing rings.

The Twist is a hybrid spanning several traditional categories: museum, bridge, and inhabitable sculpture. As a bridge, it reconfigures the Kistefos sculpture park, turning the visitor journey into a continuous loop.

The building rotates 90 degrees as it crosses the river, so that one end faces horizontally toward the forest and the other faces vertically toward the sky, creating dramatically different spatial experiences within a single compact structure.

The Spiral rises 66 stories and 1,005 feet above Hudson Yards on Manhattan's Far West Side. A cascade of terraced outdoor gardens spirals around the tower from base to top, connecting every floor with the High Line below and the sky above.
