Sustainability

Sustainable Manufacturing

The Plus is the first factory building in the world that meets the BREEAM Outstanding certification's strict environmental requirements for safe, healthy, and good physical working environments. Constructed in just 18 months, the 7,000 m² production facility is made of local PEFC-certified cross-laminated timber (CLT) and glued-laminated timber (glulam), low-carbon concrete, and recycled steel.

The factory's energy and heating demands are partly met with the help of 900 rooftop solar panels which generate about 250,000 kWh of renewable energy annually, 17 geothermal wells, and heat pumps hidden behind the walls to capture excess energy from the process of drying the components.

Net-Zero Emissions & Operations

Located on a 42-acre site at the NASA Ames Research Center, Google's Bay View campus consists of three buildings totaling 1.1 million square feet and approximately 18 acres of open space. The all-electric campus has achieved a LEED-NC v4 Platinum certification—making it the largest LEED v4 BD+C: NC Platinum certified project in the world.

On the exterior, the buildings feature a first-of-its-kind "dragonscale" solar skin roof equipped with 50,000 silver solar panels that generate a total of nearly four megawatts of energy, approximately 40% of the buildings' annual energy use.

Waste to Energy

CopenHill – officially known as the Amager Bakke – is a waste-to-energy plant that doubles as an urban recreation destination. The plant is surmounted by a ski slope, hiking trail, and climbing wall – transforming what is typically an industrial facility into a vibrant public amenity for Copenhagen residents.

The facility processes 400,000 tonnes of waste per year and generates enough electricity for 30,000 homes and district heating for 72,000 households. Every 100 kg of CO₂ emitted, a smoke ring is released from the chimney – a reminder of the environmental impact of consumption.

Climate Adaptation

The East Side Coastal Resiliency (ESCR) project protects over 110,000 New Yorkers from future storms while strengthening the neighborhood's connection to the East River. The ribbon of revitalized parkland along Manhattan's east side combines flood protection infrastructure with public amenities including sports courts, grassy lawns, an open-air amphitheater, and pedestrian bridges.

Emerging from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development's Rebuild by Design competition, ESCR demonstrates that resilience infrastructure can be beautiful, functional public space rather than blunt defensive fortification.

Biodiversity & Natural Systems

BIG's approach to landscape integrates biodiversity enhancement into every project. The Google Bay View campus includes 17.3 acres of high-value natural areas — 35% of the site is dedicated to promoting urban agriculture through native pollinator habitat, demonstration garden beds, and bee boxes while wet meadows, woodlands, and marsh contribute to broader efforts to reestablish missing essential habitat.

Our projects treat ecosystems not as constraints but as design partners, creating buildings that give more to their natural context than they take away.

The Future City

Gelephu Mindfulness City represents a new model for sustainable urbanism. Commissioned by His Majesty the King of Bhutan, the city is guided by the principle of Gross National Happiness — prioritizing ecological health, cultural preservation, and human wellbeing over economic growth alone.

With a six-story building height limit, abundant green space, and complete reliance on renewable energy, Gelephu demonstrates that the cities of the future can be more humane, more connected to nature, and more equitable than the cities of the past.
